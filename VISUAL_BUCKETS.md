# Visual Buckets - 21 Style Families

**Status:** Framework definitions complete  
**Last Updated:** 2026-05-29

---

## Purpose

This document provides detailed definitions and usage guidelines for the 21 known visual style families used to classify references and build visual direction.

---

## 1. Iridescent / Holographic Spectral

**Plain explanation:**  
Rainbow refraction, oil-slick gradients, chrome light, spectral glow, liquid glass. Optical physics applied to design.

**Mood:**  
Ethereal, futuristic, premium, dreamlike, technologically advanced.

**Color behavior:**  
Multi-hued gradients that shift based on light angle. Primary colors + unexpected secondaries. High saturation. Metallic undertones.

**Texture/material behavior:**  
Liquid, reflective, iridescent, holographic. Light-dependent. Smooth but active surfaces.

**Typography influence:**  
Glossy finishes on letterforms. Often bold or display weight. May use metallic effects.

**Motion potential:**  
Light-reactive animation. Color shifts on scroll. Refraction effects. Parallax with shifting hues.

**Image prompt potential:**  
Oil-slick surfaces, holographic materials, spectral light leaks, iridescent subjects, chrome refraction.

**Video prompt potential:**  
Color-shifting animations, light-reactive movement, holographic transitions, chromatic aberration effects.

**Best client types:**  
Tech startups, luxury brands, futuristic services, premium consumer products, entertainment.

**Bad client types:**  
Conservative businesses, institutions, traditional services, children's brands (can feel gimmicky).

**Website use cases:**  
Hero sections, transitions, accent overlays, premium product showcase, motion-heavy landing pages.

**Overuse risk:**  
Can feel dated quickly. Overuse makes text hard to read. Can look cheap if poorly executed.

**Role in site:**  
Core, Support, or Accent. Often Accent due to high visual density.

## 2. Surreal Body / Hand Abstraction

**Plain explanation:**  
Hands, body fragments, glass anatomy, symbolic human-tech tension. Human form deconstructed or abstracted.

**Mood:**  
Thoughtful, intimate, slightly unsettling, intellectual, introspective, contemporary.

**Color behavior:**  
Monochromatic or limited palette. Often skin tones + single accent. High contrast with backgrounds.

**Texture/material behavior:**  
Flesh, glass, translucency, fragmentation. Organic meets digital. Soft shadows.

**Typography influence:**  
Fine, refined letterforms. Often minimal. Pairs well with generous whitespace.

**Motion potential:**  
Subtle movement of body parts. Floating fragments. Morphing organic shapes.

**Image prompt potential:**  
Hands in meaningful poses, body fragments, glass anatomy, hand gestures, translucent flesh, symbolic body language.

**Video prompt potential:**  
Hands moving through space, body parts morphing, glass shattering/reforming, subtle organic motion.

**Best client types:**  
Creative professionals, designers, artists, mental health, wellness, luxury brands, tech humanizers.

**Bad client types:**  
Financial services (needs trust), children's products, healthcare (can feel disturbing), B2B industrial.

**Website use cases:**  
About sections, service introductions, hero features, team presentations, transitional elements.

**Overuse risk:**  
Can feel niche or pretentious. Dark interpretations needed care. May alienate conservative audiences.

**Role in site:**  
Core or Support. Less often Accent due to emotional weight.

## 3. Glitch / Digital Fragmentation

**Plain explanation:**  
Broken pixels, corrupted image data, scattered blocks, signal distortion. Intentional digital degradation.

**Mood:**  
Edgy, youthful, chaotic, playful, technological, slightly anarchic, modern.

**Color behavior:**  
Can use any color. Typically high contrast. Neon accents common. RGB channel separation.

**Texture/material behavior:**  
Digital, pixelated, fragmented, corrupted-looking. Layered overlaps. Sharp edges.

**Typography influence:**  
Can be playful or serious. Distortion effects on type. Mix of clean and broken letterforms.

**Motion potential:**  
Stuttering animations, jumping elements, glitch transitions, RGB shift effects, scan lines.

**Image prompt potential:**  
Digitally corrupted imagery, pixelated subjects, channel separation, fragmented compositions, digital artifacts.

**Video prompt potential:**  
Signal corruption animations, pixel displacement, RGB glitching, scan-line effects, stuttering transitions.

**Best client types:**  
Youth brands, music/entertainment, gaming, tech startups, creative agencies, experimental studios.

**Bad client types:**  
Banking, healthcare, conservative B2B, law firms, serious institutions (can look broken/unprofessional).

**Website use cases:**  
Transitions, loading states, hero sections (for right brands), experimental projects, motion-focused sites.

**Overuse risk:**  
Very trendy, may date quickly. Overuse feels immature. Can reduce readability. Accessibility concerns.

**Role in site:**  
Typically Accent or rare Support. Rarely Core (only for very specific brands).

## 4. Optical Geometry

**Plain explanation:**  
Concentric rings, illusion systems, hypnotic circles, visual rhythm, geometric distortion. Op-art inspired.

**Mood:**  
Mesmerizing, mathematical, premium, sophisticated, slightly hypnotic, contemporary art gallery.

**Color behavior:**  
High contrast between geometric elements. Often monochromatic + single accent. Rhythm-driven.

**Texture/material behavior:**  
Clean lines, mathematical precision, flat or slightly 3D effects. Sharp definitions.

**Typography influence:**  
Geometric sans-serif. Often structured. Can use bold weights for hierarchy.

**Motion potential:**  
Pulsing concentric rings, rotating patterns, morphing geometry, zoom effects, pattern animations.

**Image prompt potential:**  
Concentric circles, optical illusions, geometric patterns, mathematical forms, precision engineering subjects.

**Video prompt potential:**  
Rotating geometric patterns, expanding/contracting rings, morphing geometry, hypnotic animations.

**Best client types:**  
Design studios, tech companies, luxury brands, premium services, creative consultancies, galleries.

**Bad client types:**  
Casual/friendly brands, children's products, healthcare (can be unsettling), budget services.

**Website use cases:**  
Hero sections, section dividers, background patterns, accent graphics, premium landing pages.

**Overuse risk:**  
Can feel cold or mathematical. May distract from content. Accessibility concerns (flashing patterns).

**Role in site:**  
Core or Support. Can work as Accent for geometric highlights.

**Image prompt potential:**  
Photocopied imagery, grain effects, collage elements, hand-drawn illustrations, rough typography, scanned materials.

**Video prompt potential:**

**Bad client types:**

**Role in site:**  
Core for right brands. Support or Accent for others.

Core for action/adventure brands. Accent for others seeking excitement.

---

## Usage Guidelines

### Choosing a Core Style

- Best fit with client type
- Most referenced by client
- Solves primary design challenge
- Not overused in client's industry
- Sustainable across all site sections
- Supports brand identity

### Choosing a Support Style

- Complements core without competing
- Provides visual variety
- Supports secondary brand attributes
- Can transition between sections
- Adds dimension to core style

### Choosing a Rare-Use Accent

- Creates signature moment or identity
- Used sparingly (1-3 places on site)
- High visual impact
- Surprising but intentional
- Enhances memorability
- Can be most adventurous choice

---

## Style Combination Rules

- Core + Support: Always works
- Core + Accent: Highest impact combinations
- Support + Accent: Only when both serve clear purpose
- Avoid: More than 3 primary styles per site

---

## Anti-Patterns

- Mixing too many families (creates visual chaos)
- Using same family across all sections (becomes monotonous)
- Choosing style by trend rather than client fit
- Ignoring "bad client types" for a family
- Using a family without proper execution
